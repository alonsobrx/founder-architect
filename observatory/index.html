02

Core findings

1. Disability-compensation demand is institutionally large and medically complex

The FY2025 compensation report identifies 6.34 million compensation recipients, equal to 36.04 percent of the report’s 17.59 million living-veteran estimate. VA recognized 46.50 million service-connected disabilities—an average of 7.34 per recipient. Official

VA reported 1.85 million recipients at a 100-percent combined disability rating. That is 29.15 percent of compensation recipients and, by calculation, 10.50 percent of the living-veteran estimate. Calculated

2. The FY2027 claims plan has little operating reserve

VA projects 7,205,120 incoming rating and non-rating matters and 7,365,039 completions. Planned production exceeds projected receipts by 159,919 matters—only 2.22 percent of receipts—while year-end inventory remains 923,890 matters. Official Calculated

The implied planned utilization is 97.83 percent. A five-percent receipt surge would exceed planned production by 200,337 matters; a ten-percent surge would create a 560,593-matter shortfall. Calculated

3. The FY2027 Board plan increases pending appeals

The budget projects 113,433 appeals received, 102,000 decisions, and 194,191 appeals pending at year-end. The Board requests 1,163 FTE—137 fewer than the FY2026 enacted estimate and 235 fewer than FY2025 actual staffing. Official

Using FY2025 observed average output only as a sensitivity assumption, the Board would need at least 1,280 FTE to keep pace with FY2027 receipts, 1,562 FTE to reduce inventory by 25,000, or 1,844 FTE to reduce it by 50,000. Scenario

4. Accredited representation exists at scale, but capacity is not measured adequately

GAO identified 13,670 accredited individuals as of November 2024: 8,141 VSO representatives, 5,008 attorneys, and 521 claims agents. GAO also reported that three-fourths of approximately one million open disability claims in August 2024 used an accredited representative. Official

Those counts do not reveal who actively practices veterans law, accepts new clients, serves rural areas, provides Spanish-language service, or can accommodate disabled claimants. GAO found an accreditation backlog of almost 2,200 claims-agent applications and an ADF workforce of 12 paralegals and six attorneys. Official Unknown

5. The “367 AI systems running” claim is inaccurate

VA’s 2025 inventory contains 367 individual AI use cases: 138 pre-deployment, 21 pilot, 134 deployed, and 74 retired. It classifies 215 as high-impact, including 90 that were deployed. An inventory use case is not necessarily a unique system. Official Calculated

Within the VBA subset, this project identified 27 use cases: 12 pre-deployment, two pilot, eight deployed, and five retired. Eleven are classified high-impact; four of those are deployed. Calculated

What these data do not prove

  • The 83.19-percent claim-based accuracy measure is not a wrongful-denial rate.
  • The public record does not count veterans with meritorious service-connected conditions who were denied, abandoned a claim, or never filed.
  • The public record does not establish that most people assisting veterans are unaccredited.
  • The AI inventory alone does not establish that a system caused a denial, bias, or due-process violation.
  • A budget request is a projection and policy proposal, not proof of future performance.

07

Petition-ready policy framework

This preview does not publish a final legal petition or claim that the preliminary staffing scenarios are adjudicated facts. It converts the evidence into six reviewable policy requests.

Capacity reserve and transparency

Require VA to publish a workload-to-staffing model, forecast error bands, occupational assumptions, and a funded surge reserve rather than planning at near-saturation.

Board stabilization

Prevent annual appeals receipts from exceeding decision capacity and fund a time-limited inventory-reduction plan with docket-specific timeliness targets.

Accreditation workforce plan

Publish the completed workforce analysis, application aging, active-representative capacity, language coverage, accessibility capacity, and geographic service gaps.

Claimant-side parity

Fund independent, accredited, multidisciplinary assistance for complex, rural, homeless, visually impaired, cognitively impaired, and Spanish-primary claimants.

High-impact AI due process

Require human sign-off, immutable audit records, model and vendor disclosure sufficient for oversight, demographic testing, accessibility testing, override reporting, notice, and an effective challenge process.

Issue-level outcome data

Publish grants, partial grants, denials, reasons, review outcomes, remands, reversals, time to finality, and deaths before final resolution without exposing personal information.

Safer legal framing

The evidence supports allegations of structural asymmetry, regulatory scarcity, contractor dependence, queueing risk, and externalized social cost. It does not, by itself, establish bribery, fraud, or criminal corruption by any named person. The public petition should preserve that distinction.

08

Methodology and correction controls

Unit discipline

A rating-bundle claim is not the same as an individual medical issue, examination, appeal, or veteran. The site labels the unit used by each source.

Projection discipline

FY2027 budget values are displayed as requests or projections, never as completed outcomes.

No false precision

Workforce and cost outputs are scenario results. Users can change every nonofficial assumption.

Reproducibility

Public JSON and CSV files contain the inputs, formulas, source locations, retrieval dates, limitations, and document checksums.

Correction procedure

A correction must identify the disputed statement, authoritative source, exact location, and requested change. Material corrections appear in the changelog.

Privacy

No individual claim file, medical record, VA file number, Social Security number, address, credential, or proprietary Ayala logic belongs in this repository.

Accessibility statement

Accessible HTML is the authoritative publication format. The project targets WCAG 2.2 Level AA but does not claim certified conformance. It uses semantic headings, landmarks, keyboard-operable controls, visible focus, high contrast, large targets, reduced-motion support, descriptive table captions, and text equivalents instead of chart-only communication.

Automated testing cannot replace disabled-user review. Manual acceptance review remains required with TalkBack and Chrome on Android, NVDA with Firefox and Chrome on Windows, VoiceOver with Safari, keyboard-only navigation, and browser zoom at 200 and 400 percent.
